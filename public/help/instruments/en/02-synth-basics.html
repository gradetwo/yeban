Synthesizer Basics

不徐不疾，得之于手而应于心。

《庄子·天道》 / Zhuangzi, "The Way of Heaven" (Tian Dao)

Translation: Neither too fast nor too slow — the hand finds it and the mind follows. Note: synth parameters have no single correct value; balance what you hear with what you feel.

This chapter explains the built-in poly synth's signal chain, its nine projectable parameters, and the exact values of the six factory presets. The parameter names, ranges, and defaults come from the engine's device projection code; values are reproduced without any processing beyond rounding.

Signal chain

The built-in synth uses one fixed-size voice pool per track, holding at most 16 sounding voices. Each voice has two oscillator branches, a four-pole ladder low-pass filter, and an amplitude envelope. Both branches draw from the same five wavetables: hollow, organ, vocal, metallic, and glass. Each branch has its own level and detune; the branches are mixed before the low-pass and envelope.

The filter is a four-pole ladder low-pass. The device clamps cutoff to between 20 Hz and 0.45 times the sample rate; resonance and drive are 0-to-1 knob values. At drive 0 the distortion stage is bit-exactly bypassed; at 1 it is +36 dB of pre-gain, distributed evenly in dB in between. The output is self-compensated, so turning up drive changes harmonics rather than overall loudness.

The amplitude envelope is a fixed ADSR: 5 ms attack, 80 ms decay, 0.7 sustain, and 50 ms release. The current interface exposes no adjustable ADSR knobs, and no LFO, modulation matrix, or separate envelope-stage controls. Do not apply control names from other synthesizers here. If these are wired later, find the corresponding parameter projection in the code first, then extend this chapter.

The nine projectable parameters

The built-in synth reads device-chain parameter names case-insensitively. The table lists the nine parameters, their valid ranges, and defaults. “Absent” and “written as 0” are different: an absent osc2\_level means the second branch is off, and an explicit 0 also means off; an absent cutoff\_hz bypasses the whole filter, while an explicit 0 is clamped to 20 Hz by the device.

Parameter & Meaning & Valid range & Default when absent \\ cutoff\_hz (alias cutoff) & Low-pass cutoff & 20 Hz--0.45×sample rate & Filter bypassed \\ resonance (alias res) & Low-pass resonance & 0--1 & 0.0 \\ drive & Filter drive/distortion & 0--1 & 0.0 \\ osc1\_wave & First-branch wavetable & 0--4 (rounded) & 0 (hollow) \\ osc2\_wave & Second-branch wavetable & 0--4 (rounded) & 0 (hollow) \\ osc1\_level & First-branch level & 0--1 & 1.0 (full) \\ osc1\_detune\_cents & First-branch detune & ±4800 cents & 0.0 \\ osc2\_level & Second-branch level & 0--1 & 0.0 (off) \\ osc2\_detune\_cents & Second-branch detune & ±4800 cents & 0.0 \\ The nine parameters of the built-in poly synth

The wavetable index mapping is fixed: 0 = hollow, 1 = organ, 2 = vocal, 3 = metallic, 4 = glass. Non-finite or negative values are treated as 0; integers above 4 are clamped by the device to the last table, not turned into silence. When a branch level is 0, that branch executes no samples at all; this is a bit-exact bypass, not a multiply by zero.

Tables tab:preset-filter and tab:preset-osc give each preset its character directly: Hollow Lead is a 90%-level hollow branch plus a low-level second branch of the same table, suited to a soft lead; Warm Organ opens both organ branches and uses detune for organ-like thickness; Choir Pad uses vocal as the main branch and hollow as the auxiliary branch, and its pad character comes from the vocal table combined with a lower cutoff; FM Bell and Glass Pluck both use metallic/glass tables at high cutoff, the former brighter and the latter shorter; Sub Bass pushes cutoff down to 420 Hz and keeps only the low end.

Three landmark synths: three characters of subtractive synthesis

A parameter table tells you what is there now; it does not tell you why to play it that way. Three instruments in the history of subtractive synthesis map onto this chapter's three moves: the Minimoog turns “less” into weight, the Juno turns “thin” into width, and the TB-303 turns “one line” into groove.

The Minimoog (1970) packed the room-sized modular Moog into a portable instrument: three oscillators, a ladder low-pass, and no recallable presets. Its lesson is not “more parameters are better”, but “every branch needs a job”. This chapter has only two branches, which is exactly the exercise: let osc1 establish the line, then use osc2's level and detune for thickness; leave the third layer to arrangement instead of forcing it into one patch. Yeban's Sub Bass, with a 420 Hz low-pass and one organ branch, is another way of writing “less”.

The Juno-60/Juno-106 (1982/1984) used DCOs and chorus to turn “thin” into “wide”. Its chorus does not add a real third oscillator; it modulates time and stereo placement. This chapter has no chorus effect, so you can only use small detune between two branches of the same table, say $5$ to $9$ cents, and leave the rest of the width to later effects. Do not claim this equals the Juno chorus; it is an approximation with the available parameters, not a replica.

The TB-303 (1981) was designed as a single-oscillator-plus-filter bass instrument for bass players, but the dance floor turned it into an acid lead. It reminds us that resonance and cutoff are not decoration: they change pitch perception and groove. Push resonance from 0.2 to 0.5 and the harmonics around cutoff are emphasised, so the melodic centre of gravity moves; that is not volume, and drive should not be used to compensate. Yeban has no 303 glide or accent controls; what you can practise is the judgment of “one line plus one filter”.

The common lesson: make one line clear first, then add the second; listen to phase and detune before talking about thickness. Parameters are hands; listening is the ear. Do not let a preset name listen for you.
