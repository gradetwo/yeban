Space Effects

乐者，天地之和也；礼者，天地之序也。

《礼记·乐记》 / The Book of Rites, "Yue Ji" (Record of Music)

Translation: Music is the harmony of heaven and earth; ritual is the order of heaven and earth. Note: reverb and delay place a sound in a space, and give that space an order.

A space effect answers "what room is this sound in": reverb adds a room, plate, hall, or spring tail; delay adds countable echoes. Both can live inside a track's insert chain or as a shared send-and-aux-return space. Yeban currently has exactly one one-click reverb, Freeverb; convolution reverb and ping-pong delay have full parameter contracts but no manual install button in the UI. This chapter writes "principle + current state" and does not turn contract parameters into knobs that do not exist.

Four readings of reverb

Reverb is not a tail that should simply be as long as possible. It is three periods stacked: early reflections after the direct sound, the dense late reverberation that follows, and damping, where high frequencies die before lows. Pre-delay pushes the whole wet signal later so the direct sound can establish itself first; decay time sets how long the tail lasts; damping sets how dark it is; wet/dry sets how much source versus space.

These readings interact. Too much pre-delay and the tail detaches from the source; too much decay and dense passages blur; too little damping and the tail rings like metal; too much wet and the source retreats behind a wall. Tuning a space effect is not turning all four up; first choose the size of the space, then choose how far the source sits from the listener.

Insert versus send

Insert mode places the effect directly in the track's insert chain: the whole track passes through it, and the wet/dry balance is the effect's own wet control. It suits "this track needs this space", such as a vocal, a snare, or a guitar. The cost is one reverb per track, and because the effect is inside the insert chain, later changes to track gain also change the wet signal.

Send mode takes a copy of the source signal to an aux return track, where the reverb lives; the return feeds the bus. Several tracks can share one return, and the send gain is "how much each track sends in". It suits a drum kit, a backing-vocal group, or a whole instrument group sharing one space. The cost is that the return's reverb is shared, so one track cannot ask for a different room.

Yeban exposes both paths: a selected track can "install reverb", and the master bus can also "install reverb"; the send strip can "create send", whose destination is the first aux return in the project, with send gain from −60 to +12 dB. The device rack's "+ Aux Return" creates a return track. A send enters the return and then passes through the return's own insert chain — whatever is installed on the return processes everything sent into it.

The three space devices Yeban actually provides

Device & DSP & UI reachability \\ Freeverb & 8 combs + 4 allpasses per channel, mono input summed, stereo wet width & "Install reverb" one-click on the selected track; master bus has "install reverb". Parameters are read-only, no knobs \\ Convolution reverb & Synthesizes an IR from decay time and seed, then convolves; does not load an external IR file & No "install convolution" button; only via project/MCP generic device insert; the device card shows recognized parameters \\ Ping-pong delay & Alternating left/right echoes, feedback capped at 0.95, chain end after convolution & No manual "install delay" button; factory drum styles add one automatically, or via MCP \\ Space devices and their real entry points

The insert chain order is fixed: channel strip → tape saturation → Freeverb → convolution reverb → ping-pong delay. Delay is always after reverb; reverb and convolution both receive the signal after the channel strip and tape. This order is not a suggestion; it is the actual device projection order.

Freeverb parameters

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ reverb\_size & Decay length (legacy normalized control) & 0–1 & 0.45 \\ reverb\_kind & 0 Room / 1 Plate / 2 Hall / 3 Spring & 0–3 & 0 \\ reverb\_decay\_s & T60 reverb time (s); 0 = unset, use size & 0–10 & 0 \\ reverb\_damp & High-frequency damping in the tail & 0–1 & 0.35 \\ reverb\_wet & Wet path level; dry = 1 − wet & 0–1 & 0.25 \\ reverb\_predelay & Wet-path pre-delay (s) & 0–0.1 & 0.012 \\ Freeverb parameter contract (insert)

reverb\_size and reverb\_decay\_s do not add up: when decay\_s > 0, feedback is determined by the T60 calibration and size does not participate; only when decay\_s = 0 does the legacy size formula apply. To set reverb time in seconds, write a positive decay\_s; do not use size to "top it up".

reverb\_kind changes the delay-line layout and allpass feedback: Room, Plate, Hall, and Spring share the same Freeverb topology; Spring is a parameter-set approximation, not a physical spring model. Changing the type relayouts the delay lines, so density and color change, but it does not become another algorithm.

reverb\_damp only affects the tail's high frequencies: larger values make the tail darker and shorter; it does not touch the direct sound. reverb\_predelay delays only the wet path; the dry sound stays put. 0.012 s is the default 12 ms; 0.1 s is 100 ms. reverb\_wet is the wet level; the DSP takes dry as 1 − wet. It is not an independent dry/wet control.

Warning: Naming trap: reverb\_mix and reverb\_width are spellings the engine deliberately does not recognize. For wet/dry use reverb\_wet (or alias reverb\_wet\_mix); width is not exposed in the contract (internal default 0.8), so reverb\_width cannot be used as a knob. A wrong name appears in the device card's "unrecognized parameters" list and the sound does not change.

Convolution reverb parameters

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ conv\_predelay\_s & Wet-path pre-delay (s) & 0–0.1 & 0 \\ conv\_dry & Dry level (linear) & 0–1 & 1 \\ conv\_wet & Wet level (linear) & 0–1 & 0.25 \\ conv\_ir\_gain\_db & IR gain (dB) & −60–24 & 0 \\ conv\_ir\_decay\_s & Exponential decay constant of the synthesized IR (s) & 0.005–5 & 0.35 \\ conv\_ir\_seed & Random seed for the synthesized IR & $0$–$2^{32}-1$ & 1 \\ conv\_ir\_stereo & 0 = same IR L/R; non-zero = independent IR & 0/1 & 0 \\ Convolution reverb parameter contract (insert)

The convolution IR is not an external file; the engine synthesizes it from conv\_ir\_decay\_s and conv\_ir\_seed. Decay is an exponential time constant, not T60. Changing the seed changes random detail in the IR, not room size. conv\_ir\_stereo is baked at construction: 0 means both channels use the same IR; non-zero means the right channel uses a second IR derived from seed plus a golden-ratio constant. It is not automatable; changing it rebuilds the device.

The UI has no "install convolution" button, so this parameter table is currently for project/MCP writes and later wiring, not for a user turning knobs on the device card. The card can show an existing convolution device and its recognized parameters, but it will not create one out of nothing.

Ping-pong delay parameters

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ delay\_time\_s & Delay time (s) & 0.001–2 & 0.3 \\ delay\_feedback & Feedback amount & 0–0.95 & 0.3 \\ delay\_mix & Wet level; 0 = stage not built & 0–1 & 0 \\ delay\_damp & High-frequency damping in the feedback loop & 0–1 & 0 \\ delay\_ping\_pong & Ping-pong switch (non-zero = true) & 0/1 & 1 \\ delay\_sync\_enabled & Tempo-sync switch & 0/1 & 0 \\ delay\_sync\_num & Sync numerator (beats) & 1–16 & 3 \\ delay\_sync\_den & Sync denominator (beats) & 1–16 & 8 \\ delay\_sync\_dotted & Dotted switch (time ×1.5) & 0/1 & 1 \\ Ping-pong delay parameter contract (insert, chain end)

delay\_mix = 0 means the stage is not built; that is "off by default", not bypass. When delay\_sync\_enabled = 1, the delay time is converted from project tempo at projection time and delay\_time\_s no longer applies directly; the result is clamped to 2.0 s. delay\_sync\_dotted = 1 multiplies the time by 1.5, so "3/8 dotted" is 3/8 × 1.5 beats, not 3/8 beat.

Setting up a space in Yeban

  1. Decide insert or send first. For one track with its own space, select the track and click "install reverb". For a shared space across a drum kit or a vocal group, click "+ Aux Return", install reverb on the return, then click "create send" on the source track.
  2. For insert mode, set reverb\_wet first so source and tail balance. For send mode, set send gain first, then the return's reverb wet. The "wet/dry" lives in different places; do not apply one number to the other.
  3. Set reverb\_predelay: short pre-delay keeps the tail close, long pre-delay lets the source speak first; it only delays the wet path and never moves the whole track.
  4. Prefer reverb\_decay\_s when you want a time in seconds; use reverb\_size only for quick auditioning. Do not treat them as additive.
  5. Finish with reverb\_damp. If the tail is bright and hard, raise damping; if it is dull and short, lower damping; do not use wet to add brightness.

Common mistakes

  • Using reverb\_mix as the parameter name. The engine does not recognize it; the correct spelling is reverb\_wet. A wrong name does not error, it only lands in "unrecognized parameters" and the sound is unchanged.
  • Pulling both reverb\_size and reverb\_decay\_s. They are not additive: when decay\_s > 0, size does not participate. Use decay\_s for seconds, size for quick auditioning.
  • Treating pre-delay as a whole-track offset. It delays only the wet path; the dry sound does not move, and the track does not arrive late.
  • Treating reverb\_wet as a dry/wet ratio. It is the wet level; dry is automatically 1 − wet. More wet means less dry.
  • Installing a reverb on every track and then complaining the space is inconsistent. Insert mode is for per-track spaces; for a shared space, use one aux return plus sends.
  • Treating convolution reverb as "load an IR file". Yeban's convolution IR is synthesized from decay time and seed; there is no external IR import entry.
  • Treating delay\_mix = 0 as bypass. It means the stage is not built; the device may still appear on the card, but it is not in the chain.

Exercise

  1. Choose a vocal and make the same space once with an insert reverb and once through an aux return send; keep the dry signal unchanged and compare how the wet signal blends.
  2. Change reverb\_predelay from 0 to 0.03 s when a project/MCP write path is available; listen to the separation of direct sound and tail. Do not pretend there is a device-card knob.
  3. In a dense section, shorten reverb\_decay\_s or lower reverb\_wet; compare “smaller space” with “just quieter.”
