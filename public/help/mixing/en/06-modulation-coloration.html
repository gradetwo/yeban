Modulation and Coloration

木直中绳，輮以为轮，其曲中规。

《荀子·劝学》 / Xunzi, "Encouraging Learning" (Quan Xue)

Translation: Straight wood shaped against the line becomes a wheel, and its curve matches the compass. Note: modulation and coloration deliberately reshape time, phase, pitch, or harmonics.

"Modulation" makes time, phase, or pitch move slowly; "coloration" adds harmonics and texture through nonlinearity, quantization, or filtering. The two often appear together, but they are not the same: chorus is modulated delay, distortion/saturation is static nonlinearity, bit crushing is quantization error. Yeban currently has no built-in chorus, flanger, or phaser, and no separate distortion pedal; what it does have is tape saturation, soft clipping, bit crushing, plus adjacent devices such as transient shaping, sample delay, and a noise source. This chapter writes "principle + current state" and does not turn a category name into an existing device.

Three principles

Type & Method & Sound \\ Modulated delay & An LFO changes the length of a short delay line; shorter/slower reads as chorus, more feedback/faster reads as flanger & Width, softness, or comb-like movement \\ Static nonlinearity & Feed the signal into tanh, a soft knee, or hard clipping to create new harmonics & Saturation, compression, a harder edge; odd harmonics read "hard", even read "warm" \\ Quantization/ downsampling & Reduce bit depth or effective sample rate & Grain, steps, old-sampler "dirt" \\ The three principles of modulation and coloration

Chorus, flanger, and phaser all belong to the first type; they differ only in delay length, LFO rate, feedback, and dry/wet. Yeban has none of these three built in. Distortion, overdrive, and amp simulation belong to the second type; Yeban has no separate distortion pedal or cabinet simulation — soft clipping and tape saturation are the two real members of that family. Bit crushing belongs to the third type and does exist. Writing "chorus is a kind of modulated delay" is more accurate than writing "chorus is a kind of reverb".

Tape saturation

Tape saturation is Yeban's only built-in saturation device. It is a chain-level device: it is built only when `tape_drive > 0` and `tape_mix > 0`; if either is zero, it is not built, and there is no separate enabled switch.

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ tape\_drive & Saturation drive $g=1+9d$ & 0–1 & 0 \\ tape\_mix & Dry/wet; 0 = dry passthrough & 0–1 & 0 \\ tape\_trim\_db & Output trim & −24–24 dB & 0 \\ tape\_hf\_hz & Wet-path one-pole low-pass; $$20000 bypasses & 2000–20000 Hz & 20000 \\ tape\_asym & Curve asymmetry; 0 = odd only, >0 adds even & 0–0.5 & 0 \\ tape\_wow\_rate\_hz & Transport wow rate & 0.05–2 Hz & 0.5 \\ tape\_wow\_depth\_ms & Wow depth; 0 = no modulation & 0–8 ms & 0 \\ tape\_flutter\_rate\_hz & Transport flutter rate & 2–15 Hz & 6 \\ tape\_flutter\_depth\_ms & Flutter depth; 0 = no modulation & 0–2 ms & 0 \\ tape\_seed & Modulation seed (integer part) & 0–16777215 & 0 \\ Tape saturation parameters

The curve is `pade_tanh(g·x)/g`; small-signal gain is about 1. `tape_trim_db` compensates the post-saturation level loss, not "more drive". `tape_hf_hz` low-passes only the wet path; 20 kHz bypasses that low-pass and does not change drive.

`tape_asym > 0` produces even harmonics, the source of "warmth", but only when the stage is built; small-signal gain drops by about 1 dB, so use trim. `tape_wow` / `tape_flutter` are transport wow and flutter: they modulate the whole signal (dry included), not a chorus-style wet-only modulated delay. Their phase and drift are pure functions of `(seed, frame)`, so the same seed renders bit-identically. The tape stage has 31 samples of oversampling round-trip latency, reported into the device and included in PDC.

Warning: The channel strip also has `filter_drive`; that is filter drive, not tape saturation, and it does not apply to sampler tracks. To saturate a sampler track, use the tape stage; do not treat `filter_drive` as "sampler saturation".

Soft clipping and bit crushing

The soft clipper is a static nonlinearity: linear below the knee, asymptotic to a ceiling above it. It is not a distortion pedal; it has no tone control and no cabinet simulation, only drive, ceiling, knee, dry/wet, and oversampling. It sits after tape and before reverb; in the master chain it is the middle stage of "tape → clipper → limiter".

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ clipper\_drive\_db & Input drive; how much crest is clipped & 0–24 dB & 6 \\ clipper\_ceiling\_db & Asymptotic ceiling; full wet never exceeds it & −6–0 dB & −1 \\ clipper\_knee\_db & Soft-knee width; 0 near-hard, 6 softest & 0–6 dB & 3 \\ clipper\_mix & Dry/wet parallel; 1 = full wet & 0–1 & 1 \\ clipper\_oversample & Oversampling (only 1/2/4/8) & 1–8× & 4 \\ Soft clipper parameters

Bit crushing is "quantize, then sample-and-hold downsampling": `crush_bits` is bit depth, `crush_down` is the hold divisor, `crush_aa` mixes a one-pole low-pass before and after the divider to suppress aliasing. It sits after tape and before the clipper; it is a medium-degradation stage, not modulation.

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ crush\_bits & Bit depth; 16 near-transparent, 8 classic, 4 near-square & 4–16 bit & 16 \\ crush\_down & Sample-and-hold divisor; effective rate divided by it & 1–64× & 1 \\ crush\_aa & Anti-alias amount; 0 raw aliasing, 1 two-stage low-pass & 0–1 & 0 \\ Bit crusher parameters

Warning: The crusher's default 16 bit / 1 / 0 is not bit-transparent: quantization really changes the low bits. As soon as the device is projected, it changes the output; do not treat it as "transparent by default".

Adjacent devices that are not modulation

- Transient shaping: fast/slow envelope difference; `transient_attack` / `transient_sustain` each −1–1, 0 is identity; it sits after channel strip and before tape. It is dynamic shaping, not modulation. - Sample delay: pure delay, no feedback; `sample_delay_samples` 0–48000, `sample_delay_invert` polarity flip; it is manual phase alignment, not chorus. - Noise source: additive source at the chain head, hiss + crackle, seed-reproducible; it is lofi "medium noise", not modulation. - Filter drive: the channel strip's `filter_drive`, drive before the filter; it only applies to non-sampler tracks and is part of the filter stage.

None of these has a manual "install" button; they are written by the project/MCP, or installed automatically by factory drum styles on generated tracks. The device card can show an existing device and its recognized parameters, but there are no knobs to turn.

Built-in devices Yeban does not have

Third-party VST3 effects are another path: the plugin panel can scan and load effects, and offline rendering executes the first non-bypassed bound effect. They are not "built-in Yeban effects" and do not execute in real-time playback; this chapter does not count third-party plugins as built-ins.

How to use and listen

  1. Decide first whether you need "time movement" or "harmonic change". For chorus/ flanger/ phaser, Yeban has no built-in; for saturation/clipping/grain, use tape/clipper/crusher.
  2. For tape, set `tape_mix` until you can hear the blend, then push `tape_drive`; use `tape_trim_db` to restore level, not the fader.
  3. For "warm", add `tape_asym`; for "hard", leave it at 0. For brighter, raise `tape_hf_hz`; for darker, lower it.
  4. For the clipper, set `clipper_ceiling_db` first, then push `clipper_drive_db`; a larger `clipper_knee_db` is softer, lower `clipper_mix` keeps transients.
  5. For the crusher, start at `crush_bits=8`, `crush_down=2`; `crush_aa=0` is dirtiest, 1 is smoothest. Do not max all three at once.
  6. None of these stages has a manual entry; if the device card does not show it, do not assume "there is another one in the menu". Generate with a drum style or write via MCP, then audition.

Common mistakes

  • Treating chorus as a built-in Yeban effect. There is no built-in chorus, flanger, or phaser; only third-party VST3 effects or external production.
  • Treating soft clipping as a distortion pedal. It has no tone control and no cabinet model; it is only soft-knee clipping.
  • Treating the channel strip's filter\_drive as sampler saturation. It does not apply to sampler tracks; use tape for sampler saturation.
  • Treating tape wow/flutter as chorus. It modulates the whole signal (dry included), not a wet-only modulated delay.
  • Treating the crusher default as transparent. 16 bit / 1 / 0 still changes low bits; if the device is projected, the output changes.
  • Judging coloration by real-time monitoring. The real-time engine wires only part of the stages; final timbre of transient/crusher/clipper/noise is defined by offline export.
  • Counting third-party VST3 effects as built-ins. They are a plugin path, execute offline, first-one-wins; not the same entry as built-in devices.

Exercise

  1. Choose drums or bass. Move tape\_drive from 0 to 0.3, then tape\_mix from 0 to 0.5; use tape\_trim\_db to restore level.
  2. A/B at the same monitoring level: transients first, then low end, then noise/sibilance. Write “changed/unchanged” per item; do not just write “warmer.”
  3. Remember that the real-time chain and the offline export chain are not fully identical; do not judge export coloration from real-time monitoring.
