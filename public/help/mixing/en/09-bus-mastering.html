Introduction to Buses and Mastering

治大国若烹小鲜。

《老子·第六十章》 / Laozi, Chapter 60

Translation: Ruling a large state is like cooking a small fish. Note: mastering is a light touch on the master bus; too much stirring breaks the mix.

A bus is where several tracks meet and get processed together; mastering is the final bus pass and delivery check before the song leaves the building. Yeban has no separate mastering suite. It has three real entry points: auxiliary return tracks (AuxReturn), the master bus (Master) with its fader and insert chain, and the export dialog with its bus limiter, format settings and post-export measurement line. Loudness targets, true-peak ceilings and loudness normalization exist only on the CLI/MCP path; the GUI has no fields for them, and this chapter does not pretend otherwise.

Signal flow from tracks to file

The bus path can be reduced to one sentence: track device chain $$ track fader/pan $$ send edge $$ auxiliary return $$ master sum $$ master fader $$ master insert chain $$ export limiter (optional) $$ quantization/dither $$ WAV.

The master fader sits before the insert chain, not after it. Push the fader up and the level entering EQ, compression and reverb rises with it; the export limiter is the last stage after the inserts. Do not use the master fader to compensate a limiter, and do not treat it as mastering gain: the fader changes the drive into the insert chain, while the limiter changes the final ceiling.

Auxiliary returns and sends

An auxiliary return track has kind AuxReturn; it exists for summing and shared processing, not for hosting an instrument. There are two creation entries:

  • the track menu item “+ New auxiliary bus track”;
  • the device rack's “+ Aux return” button in the send area.

Once it exists, open a display track's device rack, click “New send”, and choose that auxiliary return as the destination. A send edge carries one gain field, gain\_db; there is no pre/post switch. The UI has no “send point before/after fader” choice, so do not assume another DAW's pre/post convention.

Auxiliary returns are for processing shared by several tracks:

Use & Where & Reason \\ Shared reverb & Reverb on the aux return; level via the send-edge gain & Several tracks feed one reverb and share one tail; do not insert an identical reverb on every track \\ Shared delay & Delay on the aux return & One delay time/feedback for alignment \\ Group compression & Several tracks into one aux return, then to master & Compress only that group, not the whole song; the aux fader still rides the group \\ Common uses of auxiliary returns

For a shared reverb, set the reverb device to mostly wet and little dry; the dry signal still travels directly from the source track to the master. Otherwise the same dry signal appears again in the aux return and becomes double dry. The send-edge gain is absolute dB, not a percentage; lowering it lowers the level into the reverb but does not change the source track's direct path.

The master bus chain

The master bus track has kind Master. Select it and press F9 to open the device rack and see its insert chain; press X or F8 to open the mixer console and see the master fader, meters and channel strip.

What the master bus can do:

Stage & Entry point & Do not use it for \\ Summing & the routing graph feeds every track/aux return into Master & fixing one track's frequency response or dynamics \\ Fader & master fader in the mixer console (F8/X) & replacing a limiter or loudness normalization \\ Insert chain & EQ, Compressor, Reverb, Delay, Limiter, etc.\ on the master device rack & stacking a long “mastering preset”; every device needs a stated reason \\ Export limiter & the bus limiter switch in the export dialog & an adjustable mastering limiter; it is fixed and per-export \\ What each master-bus stage is for, and what it is not for

Bus processing starts with “fix the source first, then fix the bus”. A single track that is too bright, too loud or too bass-heavy should be fixed on that track's EQ/compressor/fader; the master should only handle problems shared by every track: overall dynamics, overall tonal balance, overall space. Pulling 3 dB of low end on the master is often paying for excess low end on several tracks, and the fix will not transfer to the next arrangement.

Three common bus stages

  1. Master EQ: small corrections only. Use the channel-strip low cut to remove inaudible/DC-adjacent energy below 20--30 Hz; move the mid/high region by 1--2 dB only when the whole mix is too bright or too dark. Do not use the master to sweep for a single track's problem.
  2. Master compression: use Compressor as glue, usually only 1--3 dB of gain reduction; start around 2:1, with an attack that does not grab transients and a release that does not pump. The compressor is in the insert chain and the fader is before it, so pushing the fader changes the compressor input; set the fader first, then the threshold.
  3. Master limiting: the Limiter device has threshold, ceiling, release, lookahead and a true-peak switch; the export dialog's bus limiter is a different fixed switch. Do not leave both on without knowing it, or you get two limiting stages and the second one is not adjustable.

Two limiters are not the same limiter

& Device-chain Limiter & Export-dialog limiter & Loudness normalization \\ Position & inside the master insert chain & after the master fader/ automation, before quantization & after rendering, before quantization \\ Parameters & threshold/ ceiling/ release/ lookahead/ true peak & fixed 0.9 threshold, 0.95 ceiling, 33-frame lookahead, true-peak detection off & target LUFS + true-peak ceiling (CLI/MCP) \\ Default & exists only when inserted & off & no target, no action \\ Adjustable & device parameters & on/off only & target and ceiling \\ Stored in project & yes (device chain) & no (per-export modifier) & no (per-export modifier) \\ Device-chain limiter, export limiter and loudness normalization are different things

The export-dialog limiter has a 33-frame lookahead; the export path compensates those 33 frames, so output length and timeline do not change. Its ceiling of linear 0.95 is about $-0.45$ dBFS and its threshold of 0.9 is about $-0.92$ dBFS. It does not do true-peak oversampling, so it cannot guarantee “true peak below $-1$ dBTP”; for that guarantee, read the post-export measurement or use the CLI/MCP loudness normalization/true-peak ceiling.

Loudness readings: UI and export are not the same

Yeban's UI shows the letters “LUFS”, but the live readings and the export readings must be kept apart:

Reading & Source & Can it be a delivery reference? \\ Mixer master LUFS & master smoothed RMS minus 0.691 dB & only a rough level guide; not BS.1770 gated integrated loudness, so do not report “the song is $-14$ LUFS” from it \\ Control-room short-term LUFS & same master reading & same; it has no 3 s short-term window and no gating \\ Control-room integrated LUFS & no runtime update in the current build; only the UI default string & not a reading; do not screenshot it as evidence \\ Control-room dBTP & no runtime update; only the UI default string & not a reading; true peak exists only in the post-export measurement \\ Post-export measurement & 16$$ true peak, gated integrated LUFS, LRA & the only usable delivery reading; unmeasurable values are printed as “not measured” \\ LUFS in the UI versus the post-export measurement

This is not nitpicking about names. The master reading is “smoothed RMS minus 0.691 dB”, without BS.1770 K-weighting, gating, or the 400 ms/3 s windows. It can tell you roughly how loud the program is, but it cannot prove “this song reaches $-14$ LUFS”. The control room's integrated LUFS and dBTP fields have no runtime writer in the current build; their default strings are placeholders. To get a delivery reading, export once and read the measurement line.

The three numbers in the post-export line are:

  • True peak: a dBFS reading from 16$$ oversampling; 0 is full scale, positive values are over.
  • Integrated loudness: BS.1770-4 gated integrated LUFS; programs shorter than 400 ms, or entirely below the absolute gate, show “not measured”.
  • LRA: P95$-$P10 of the 3 s short-term histogram with a $-20$ LU relative gate; shorter than 3 s, or no short-term value above the gate, shows “not measured”.

Export options and loudness targets

The WAV export dialog has five real controls:

Control & Default & Semantics \\ Output path & none & browse or type; filling the path does not export \\ Sample rate & auto & follows the project; it does not display the project rate, so do not read auto as a fixed 48 kHz \\ Bit depth & 24 & 16/24-bit integers use TPDF dither; 32f floats pass through without dither \\ Seconds & audible project end & seeded when the dialog opens; clearing it falls back to the 8 s default, not the whole song. Check long tails and silent tails yourself \\ Bus limiter & off & a per-export boolean; fixed parameters; not stored in the project, not undoable \\ The real controls in the WAV export dialog

The GUI has no controls for: loudness target (LUFS), true-peak ceiling, peak normalization, dither mode, noise shaping or mastering clipper. They are not in the export dialog; do not write a GUI procedure that claims “set $-14$ LUFS here”.

Only the CLI/MCP path has loudness normalization:

Preset & Target LUFS & True-peak ceiling & Entry \\ Streaming & $-14$ & $-1$ dBTP & CLI/MCP; not the GUI \\ EBU R128 & $-23$ & $-1$ dBTP & CLI/MCP; not the GUI \\ Built-in loudness presets; the numbers are product choices, not a normative mandate

MCP rendering's targetLufs accepts $[-70,0]$; when supplied, it applies gain to reach the target, with a true-peak ceiling of $-1.0$ dBTP as the binding limit. It re-measures with a $0.5$ LU tolerance and fails loudly with RENDER\_FAILED if the target is missed; it never silently ships an off-target file. The CLI also has --true-peak-ceiling for setting the ceiling independently. Loudness normalization is available only at 44.1/48/88.2/96 kHz; other sample rates fail instead of silently falling back to 48 kHz coefficients.

For a range export the target is measured on the exported slice, not the whole song. A stem target and a full-song target are not the same thing; do not apply the full song's $-14$ to an eight-second stem and ask why the reading differs.

Pre-export checklist

  1. Check the sources: is any single track too bright, too loud or too bass-heavy? Fix it on the track, not on the master.
  2. Master fader: make sure it is not pushed far past 0 dB just to “sound louder”; it sits before the insert chain, so it changes the compressor/limiter input.
  3. Insert chain: confirm bypass states and parameters one device at a time; do not leave both the device-chain Limiter and the export limiter on without knowing it.
  4. Export once with the limiter off: read the post-export true peak, integrated LUFS and LRA as the unrestricted baseline.
  5. Clipping warning: if “master clipped N samples, reduce by X dB” appears, follow the suggestion on the master or the loudest source; do not ignore it and ship anyway.
  6. Targets: when a target is required, use the CLI/MCP targetLufs/--loudness-target, not the GUI; confirm the sample rate is supported.
  7. Format: 24-bit integer (TPDF) is the common delivery choice; use 32f when downstream processing continues; use 16-bit only when the downstream explicitly asks. Keep the project sample rate unless there is a delivery reason to change it.
  8. Duration: confirm the seconds field is not the 8 s default; include the outro, silent tail and fade.
  9. Final listen: use the F4 control room for MONO/SIDE checks; these switches do not affect export, so do not judge export level with MONO on.

Check & Pass condition \\ Master clipping & post-export true peak is within the target ceiling; if a clipping warning appears, reduce the source or the master first \\ Loudness & within $0.5$ LU when a target is set; without a target, record the measurement and do not claim compliance \\ Format & sample rate matches delivery; bit depth matches downstream; integer depths are dithered \\ Duration & covers the whole song, including the outro, not the 8 s fallback \\ Monitoring & no must-keep part disappears in MONO; no centred dry signal dominates SIDE \\ Pre-export checklist

Exercise

  1. Import a reference track to its own audio track and bypass its effects; use it only for A/B, not in the export.
  2. Export once with the bus limiter off and read the measured true peak, integrated LUFS, and LRA; export again with the limiter on and compare.
  3. Tick the pre-export checklist; at least confirm the duration is not the 8-second default, mute/solo are reset, and a must-keep part does not disappear in MONO.
