Stereo Image and Panning

有无相生，难易相成，长短相形，高下相倾，音声相和，前后相随。

《老子·第二章》 / Laozi, Chapter 2

Translation: Being and non-being generate each other; long and short define each other; high and low lean on each other; sound and voice harmonize; front and back follow. Note: stereo is relational — left only means something against right.

Pan answers “where is this sound placed left or right”, stereo width answers “how far apart are its two sides”, and mono compatibility answers “how much remains after the left and right channels are summed”. These three are often collapsed into one “image” knob, but in Yeban they live on different layers: track pan at the source, width in the channel strip's M/S stage, low-frequency narrowing in the strip's mono-maker, and mono checking in the control-room monitor path. This chapter follows those four layers and marks the controls that are not provided.

Three distinct layers

Yeban does not have one global image control. It has three independent parameter families:

Layer & Parameter / entry & Scope and boundary \\ Track & TrackV3.pan, each mixer strip's pan cell & $-1$--$+1$; every track, bus and master has its own; can be automated as TrackPan \\ Channel strip & strip\_width, mono\_below\_hz, ms\_mid\_* & Exists only when the track has a channel strip; not a width knob beside the fader \\ SFZ region & pan, width, position & Per sampler region; only in the SFZ file, no UI editor \\ The three layers of image parameters in Yeban

Track pan moves the whole track left or right; channel-strip width spreads or collapses its left/right components; an SFZ region's pan places a particular slice of the sampled instrument. They can be combined, but one cannot do the job of another.

Track pan and the four pan laws

Track pan is bounded to $-1$--$+1$: $-1$ is hard left, $0$ centre, $+1$ hard right. The UI converts the thousandths integer into readings such as C, L50 and R50: centre shows C, negative values show L plus an integer percentage, positive values R plus a percentage. The mixer strip's pan cell, the inspector and the arrangement share one value.

Pan is not a simple left/right gain pair. It follows the project-level audio\_config.pan\_law. Yeban has four laws:

Serialized name & Curve & Curve centre (before the identity rule) & Centre level \\ Linear & $L=1-p$, $R=p$, $p=(pan+1)/2$ & $(0.5,0.5)$ & $-6.02$ dB \\ ConstantPower Minus3dB & $L=$, $R=$ & $(2/2,2/2)$ & $-3.01$ dB \\ ConstantPower Minus4\_5dB & $L=^{}$, $R=^{}$ & $(0.5957,0.5957)$ & $-4.50$ dB \\ ConstantPower Minus6dB & $L=^2$, $R=^2$ & $(0.5,0.5)$ & $-6.02$ dB \\ The four pan laws; $=(pan+1)/4$, $=0.45/_{1021.4948677$}

The default is ConstantPower Minus3dB: the sum of left and right energy is exactly one, so sweeping pan does not collapse the total energy. The linear law keeps the amplitude sum at one and loses 6 dB in the centre; $-4.5$ dB and $-6$ dB are the other two constant-power curves. The project field accepts the four serialized names above, but the current UI has no pan-law selector; change audio\_config.pan\_law directly in the project JSON, and note that MCP currently has no writer for it.

Centre identity and the 3 dB step

At dead centre Yeban does not use the curve centre. It uses identity:

centered\_pan\_gains(pan, law): $pan=0$ or non-finite $ (1.0,1.0)$; otherwise pan\_gains.

So a track reading C multiplies both sides by one, and monitoring and export agree bit-for-bit. As soon as pan leaves centre it falls onto the selected curve. For the default $-3$ dB law, $pan=$ gives about $0.7071$ on each side, $3.01$ dB below centre; the step is $6.02$ dB for the linear law, about $4.50$ dB for $-4.5$ dB, and $6.02$ dB for $-6$ dB. This is Yeban's existing contract, not a typo: centre must align with the offline “pan untouched” case, and the price is an audible step between exact centre and a tiny non-zero pan.

The UI reading rounds to thousandths, so a very small non-zero pan may display C while processing already follows the curve. Do not infer “identity” from a displayed C; to reset exactly, write the value back to $0$.

Tracks, buses and automation

Tracks, auxiliary buses and the master each carry a pan, and all use the same centered\_pan\_gains. Master pan is not an output balance or a master gain; it moves the whole mix left or right. Do not use it to fix the position of one instrument.

Pan can be written as a TrackPan automation lane. When automation exists, it replaces the track's static pan, smoothed per sample with a 5 ms time constant; the static value is used only when no automation exists. See the automation chapter for drawing and reading; this chapter does not repeat it.

Width: the channel strip's M/S scaling

Yeban has no per-track width fader and no track parameter named width. Real width lives in the channel-strip device as strip\_width (alias channel\_width), range $0.0$--$2.0$, default $1.0$:

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ strip\_width & M/S width scaling; $1$ neutral, $0$ mono, $2$ double & 0.0--2.0 & 1.0 \\ mono\_below\_hz & mono-maker corner; $0$ bypass & 0--1000 Hz & 0 \\ ms\_mid\_gain\_db & peak gain on mid only & $-60$--$24$ dB & 0 \\ ms\_mid\_freq\_hz & M/S mid peak centre & 100--12000 Hz & 2000 \\ Channel-strip width and M/S parameters

The width stage computes:

mid = 0.5(L+R); side = 0.5(L-R) * strip\_width; (L,R) = (mid+side, mid-side).

At $1.0$ the stage is skipped bit-for-bit: no M/S round trip, because in $f32$ $(m+s)+(m-s)$ is not guaranteed to equal $2m$. At $0.0$ side is multiplied by zero, both channels keep only mid, and the whole track becomes mono. At $2.0$ side is doubled, the left/right difference is exaggerated and the image feels wider, while the centre and low-frequency phase relationship is unchanged. Width never touches mid, so the centre of a vocal or kick does not drift sideways.

The width stage sits inside the channel strip: input gain → high-pass → EQ → dynamic EQ → filter → compressor → width → M/S mid → mono-maker → output gain. It exists only when the track has a channel strip; without one there is no strip\_width. The UI has no width knob; the device card shows recognised parameters and a curve only. To change width, use MCP setParam with lane set to DeviceParam and address strip\_width by name, or edit the device parameters in the project. The current version does not provide a track-level width knob, a dedicated automation lane named “width”, or a width meter; automate width through the DeviceParam lane.

Low-frequency narrowing: mono-maker

mono\_below\_hz is the channel strip's stage for narrowing low-frequency side energy, range $0$--$1000$ Hz, default $0$ (bypassed). Its contract is:

mid unchanged; side through a second-order Butterworth high-pass; reconstructed to left/right.

It is not a brick-wall split: with a $120$ Hz corner, the side at $60$ Hz is about $-12$ dB, at $30$ Hz about $-24$ dB, and above $240$ Hz essentially untouched. It pins the low end to the centre; it does not make everything below $120$ Hz absolutely mono. For full-band mono, set strip\_width to $0$, not mono-maker.

Mono-maker runs after width: width scales the whole side (including low frequencies), then mono-maker removes the low-frequency side. Reversing the order leaves a different low-frequency residue. It also exists only inside a channel strip.

M/S mid: centre only, sides untouched

ms\_mid\_gain\_db is a peak EQ applied only to mid, range $-60$--$24$ dB, default $0$ (bypassed); ms\_mid\_freq\_hz ranges $100$--$12000$ Hz, default $2000$ Hz, with a fixed Q of $0.9$. It is not the ordinary EQ mid: eq.mid\_* is full-band, so boosting 3 kHz also boosts the cymbals and reverb tail in side. M/S mid boosts only the centre, bringing a lead vocal or melody forward without brightening the sides. At $0$ dB the stage is bypassed and performs no M/S round trip at all.

It is neither width nor a mono-compatibility tool. Boosting mid does not replace mono-maker; it makes the centre louder, not the low-frequency side disappear.

The SFZ region image family

An SFZ region can declare three image parameters, all in percent:

SFZ parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ pan & Region pan; placement for mono samples, balance for stereo & $-100$--$100$ & 0 \\ width & Stereo width; $100$ neutral, $0$ mono, negative swaps L/R & $-100$--$100$ & 100 \\ position & Moves the whole image after width & $-100$--$100$ & 0 \\ SFZ region image parameters

The order is pan → width → position. width and position apply only to stereo samples; a mono sample's voice does not enter the split-channel path and the values are not applied. The default combination (width=100, position=0) short-circuits bit-for-bit and performs no floating-point work. All three are clamped to $-100$--$100$ at load time; out-of-range values raise a ClampedStaticValue warning.

Yeban can read mono and stereo sample files, but the UI has no SFZ region image editor; these values exist only in the SFZ file. The current version does not provide a graphical editor for region pan/width/position.

Mono-compatibility checking

Mono compatibility is not a plug-in; it is a set of switches in the control-room monitor path. Press F4 to show the control room and loudness meters. Its right side has DIM, MONO and SIDE; the lower-dock master strip also has a MONO, which is the same boolean as the control room, not a second state.

Switch & Processing & Use & Boundary \\ MONO & both channels become $(L+R)/2$ & check cancellation and low-end thinning & monitor only, not export \\ SIDE & both channels become $L-R$ & hear the difference signal of reverb/wide sources & monitor only, not export \\ DIM & both channels multiply by $0.1$ ($-20$ dB) & temporarily lower monitoring level & monitor only, not export \\ Control-room monitor switches

The order of the three switches is a contract: SIDE first, then MONO, then DIM. Therefore SIDE and MONO together still produce the difference signal, not silence; doing MONO first and SIDE second would subtract the two copies of $(L+R)/2$ and yield zero, which Yeban does not do. With all three off the stage is skipped entirely and touches no sample.

A checking routine:

  1. Monitor normally and note where the vocal, kick and bass sit in the centre.
  2. Press MONO; listen for a thinner vocal, a kick that loses low end, or reverb that suddenly grows. An instrument that almost disappears in mono is relying on left/right difference.
  3. Press SIDE; confirm the difference signal does not contain the dry centre you want to keep. SIDE+MONO together is still the difference signal, not silence.
  4. Turn MONO/SIDE off and return to normal monitoring; use DIM if you need a lower monitoring level.

The current version does not provide automatic mono-compatibility analysis, a left/right correlation meter, a phase-cancellation warning, or a before/after mono-fold difference meter. MONO is a manual monitoring switch, not an analyser; it will not tell you “this track lost 3 dB in mono”. The export path bypasses these switches, so do not judge export level while MONO is on.

Common mistakes

Mistake & Actual behaviour \\ “Pan is width” & Pan moves the whole track; width scales only side, leaving mid alone \\ “Centre means 0 dB on the curve” & Centre is identity at 0 dB; leaving centre drops onto the curve, a $3.01$ dB step for the default law \\ “Width 0 is the same as mono-maker at 120 Hz” & Width 0 is full-band mono; mono-maker only rolls off low-frequency side at second order \\ “If an SFZ file says width, it must be audible” & It applies only to stereo samples; mono samples ignore it \\ “MONO also makes the export mono” & Monitor switches bypass export \\ “Channel-strip width can be dragged like a fader” & No width knob exists; use device parameters/MCP or the project file \\ Common mistakes about pan and stereo

Exercise

  1. Move a track's pan from C to L50, then to R50; return to the full mix after each move and listen to the center and sides.
  2. Write the pan back to a true $0$ and confirm the display reads C. Do not infer identity processing from a “C” display.
  3. Check the track with control-room MONO/SIDE; record what is lost in mono, and do not use the monitoring level as the export level.
