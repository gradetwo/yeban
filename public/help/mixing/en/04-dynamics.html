Dynamics Processing

其进锐者其退速。

《孟子·尽心上》 / Mencius, "Jin Xin I"

Translation: What advances sharply also retreats quickly. Note: compression and transient shaping control fast peaks without flattening the whole performance.

Dynamics processing changes not frequency but how level behaves over time. It makes loud peaks step back, brings quiet detail closer, or keeps unwanted sound out. Compression, limiting, gating, and expansion are four different goals; treating "loud" as the only goal usually flattens a mix.

Four devices, four goals

Device & What it does & Typical use \\ Compressor & Reduces the part above threshold by a ratio; below threshold stays untouched & Control vocal/bass dynamics; make a track sit steadily in the mix \\ Limiter & Pins peaks below a ceiling with a very high ratio and very fast attack & Prevent overs before export, or protect downstream devices \\ Gate & Attenuates or mutes the part below threshold & Remove noise floor, bleed, unwanted tails \\ Expander & Pushes the part below threshold further down; upward expansion lifts it & Restore contrast, make quieter sections quieter; upward expansion is rarer \\ The different goals of four dynamics devices

The line between compression and limiting is not the name but whether the attack lets transients through. A compressor can use a slow attack so the first transient stays intact and only the sustained part is compressed; a limiter's attack must be fast enough that the peak does not cross the ceiling. A gate is not a "reverse compressor": it acts below threshold to remove content. An expander also acts below threshold, but its goal is to widen the level difference, not to mute.

Compressor parameters

Yeban's built-in device is a feed-forward soft-knee compressor. The chain is: stereo-linked RMS detection → soft-knee static curve → independent gain attack/release → multiply back into the input, then makeup. It has no lookahead delay and does not look at the output, so there is no feedback loop.

Parameter & Meaning & Range & Default \\ threshold\_db & Where compression starts & $-120$–$24$ dBFS & $-12$ dBFS \\ ratio & Compression ratio & 1–1000 & 4:1 \\ knee\_db & Soft-knee width & 0–24 dB & 6 dB \\ detector\_s & RMS detector window time constant & 0.0001–10 s & 0.005 s \\ attack\_s & Time constant when gain moves down & 0.0001–10 s & 0.01 s \\ release\_s & Time constant for gain returning to 1.0 & 0.0001–10 s & 0.1 s \\ makeup\_db & Post-compression makeup gain & $-24$–$24$ dB & 0 dB \\ Yeban's dynamics parameter contract

All three time values are time constants $$, not "10%–90% rise time": 63.2% of the way to steady state is $$, and 1 dB from steady state is about $1.585$. The detector window answers "how fast do we see the current RMS"; attack/release answer "how fast does gain follow". Merging them into one attack knob makes the readings impossible to reconcile.

The soft-knee formula interpolates quadratically within $W/2$ above and below threshold: transparent below the knee, smooth inside, ratio compression above. $W=0$ degenerates to a hard knee with a corner at threshold. The point of a soft knee is to avoid abruptly crushing material that just crosses threshold, not to make it louder.

Where the compressor actually lives in Yeban

Click "Insert Compressor" in the device rack to add the built-in soft-knee compressor to the current track. The rack's compressor readout shows threshold, ratio, and GR; the button beside it cycles the ratio: 2:1 → 4:1 → 8:1 → 20:1. If the current ratio is not in that list, the next click starts at 2:1.

UI element & Actual behavior & Boundary \\ "Insert Compressor" & Inserts the dynamics group with contract defaults & Multiple units can be inserted on one track \\ Compressor readout & Shows threshold/ratio of the first device with ratio & Falls back to placeholder values when none is found \\ Ratio cycle button & Changes only that device's ratio & Only the four values 2/4/8/20 \\ GR readout & Always shows 0.0 dB & Not a live gain-reduction meter \\ Compressor curve & Draws a hard-knee transfer curve from threshold + ratio & Ignores knee/ detector/ attack/ release; not a soft-knee measurement \\ Threshold/ knee/ detector/ attack/ release/ makeup knobs & The contract has the parameters & The current rack has no separate controls; they cannot be dragged \\ What the compressor UI elements actually do

The two easiest misreads are "GR 0.0 dB" and the "measured curve". The GR string is fixed by the projection and does not mean there is no reduction; the curve uses the hard-knee formula $y=T+(x-T)/R$, while the device runs the three-part soft-knee formula. Judge whether compression happens by ear, meters, and the overall dynamics; do not treat the curve as a live meter.

Status of limiter, gate, and expander

Yeban's effect contract has a separate limiter group: threshold $-30$–$0$ dB (default $-1$), ceiling $-6$–$0$ dB (default $-0.3$), release 10–1000 ms (default 80), lookahead 1–192 frames. But the one-click built-in effects are only EQ, Reverb, and Compressor; there is no "Insert Limiter" entry. The UI line "Limiter: threshold -0.1 dBTP · status normal (no overs)" is fixed text, not a state read from a real limiter device.

Gate and expander have no corresponding built-in effect group and no rack entry. They can be explained as concepts, but they cannot be operated as existing features on this rack. If these processes are needed, use a path outside the current built-in contracts or wait for a later version; the manual does not write "should exist" as "exists now".

Setup order and common mistakes

  1. Fix level before compression: if a track jumps because of performance, flatten the sections with fader/automation first; compression handles the remaining peaks and dips.
  2. Set threshold against the loudest section: it should sit below the peaks that need control without compressing every phrase; a common start is 3–6 dB reduction on the loudest part.
  3. Choose ratio by goal: 2:1–4:1 suits vocal/bass dynamics; 8:1 and above feels closer to limiting and can sound squashed.
  4. Do not always use the fastest attack: too fast shaves drum/plucked transients; too slow lets peaks through. 10 ms is a common start.
  5. Do not always use the shortest release: too short pumps; too long makes the next phrase slow to recover. 100 ms is a common start, adjusted to the song.
  6. Makeup is not a loudness button: it raises everything and can clip the bus; check post-compression peaks before adding gain.

Common mistakes: using a compressor as a limiter (slow attack leaks peaks), using a limiter as a loudness tool (more limiting means flatter), using a gate to "fix" performance (it cuts tails and breaths too), trusting the fixed GR 0.0 as "no compression", and reading the hard-knee schematic as a soft-knee measurement. Yeban currently has no separate knobs for these parameters; when fine control is needed, solve it in project data or wait for later controls, and do not pretend curve dragging changes knee or attack.

Exercise

  1. Put a compressor on a track with wide dynamics. With the threshold fixed, compare 2:1 and 8:1; note transients and sustained parts.
  2. Treat the GR reading as a placeholder, not proof of “no compression”; judge by ear, meters, and overall movement.
  3. Do a “fix level first, then compress” A/B: flatten sections with fader/automation, then compress; compare pumping and detail.
