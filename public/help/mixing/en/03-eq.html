Equalization (EQ)

八音克谐，无相夺伦，神人以和。

《尚书·舜典》 / The Book of Documents, "Canon of Shun" (Shun Dian)

Translation: The eight tones can harmonize without stealing one another's place, and gods and people are brought into accord. Note: EQ shapes relative frequency relationships; it does not make every track bright.

EQ is not a magic "make it sound better" knob; it changes the relative level of frequency ranges. Cutting a 300 Hz buildup and lifting presence at 3 kHz can transform the same take, but neither replaces correct level, pan, or performance. This chapter builds a frequency map first, then explains what Yeban's three-band shaping EQ can and cannot do.

A frequency map and masking

Human hearing spans roughly 20 Hz–20 kHz. Mixing usually divides it into bands, but the divisions are not hard borders; adjacent bands always interact:

Lows carry weight and foundation; low-mids carry body; mids carry melody and speech; the presence region carries clarity; highs carry air. When two sources are loud in the same band, masking occurs: the ear follows the louder one, and the other can "disappear" even at a healthy level. EQ's first job is to reduce that competition, not to give every track a pretty curve.

Subtract first, add later

  1. Find what is excess: sweep by boosting a narrow band, locate the spot that turns muddy or harsh, then cut instead. The boost is only a probe; the final curve usually cuts more than it boosts.
  2. Add only what is missing: after subtracting, if weight or air is still missing, lift gently. A boost raises everything in that band, including noise and sibilance.
  3. Return to the full mix: a solo track sounding good does not prove it works in context. After each move, check whether other elements get pushed away.

A common sweep: push the mid gain to +9–12 dB, move the center slowly, stop at the most uncomfortable point, return the gain to 0, and cut 2–4 dB there. Yeban currently has no separate Q knob; a newly inserted EQ uses the default mid Q of 0.9, and curve dragging changes gain and center frequency only. So a truly narrow probe is not available on this rack; the probe is wider than a dedicated EQ, and judgment should rely on the whole mix rather than one frequency number.

Yeban's three-band shaping EQ

Click "Insert EQ" in the device rack to add a three-band shaping EQ to the current track's device chain. It is three series RBJ biquads: low shelf, mid peak, high shelf. When a band's gain is 0 dB, that band is mathematically identity; when all three are 0 dB, the whole EQ is a true bypass, not an approximate one.

Parameter & Role & Range & Default \\ eq\_low\_gain & Low-shelf gain & $-18$–$+18$ dB & 0 dB \\ eq\_low\_freq & Low-shelf corner & 20–1000 Hz & 200 Hz \\ eq\_mid\_gain & Mid-peak gain & $-18$–$+18$ dB & 0 dB \\ eq\_mid\_freq & Mid center & 200–8000 Hz & 1000 Hz \\ eq\_mid\_q & Mid quality factor & 0.3–6 & 0.9 \\ eq\_high\_gain & High-shelf gain & $-18$–$+18$ dB & 0 dB \\ eq\_high\_freq & High-shelf corner & 1000–16000 Hz & 4000 Hz \\ Parameter contract of Yeban's three-band shaping EQ

The response graph in the device rack is computed from real parameters, not decoration. The horizontal axis is 20 Hz–20 kHz logarithmic, the vertical axis is $18$ dB, and the center line is 0 dB. The graph's accessibility label still says "4-band EQ response", but the current contract and implementation are three bands: low shelf, one mid peak, high shelf. There is no fourth band and no fourth draggable point.

Dragging on the curve

The response graph is also a drag surface. A drag writes two parameters at once: the band's gain and its frequency.

Horizontal position & Gain written & Frequency written \\ Left third ($x<0.333$) & eq\_low\_gain & eq\_low\_freq \\ Middle third ($0.333 x<0.667$) & eq\_mid\_gain & eq\_mid\_freq \\ Right third ($x0.667$) & eq\_high\_gain & eq\_high\_freq \\ Curve-drag destinations

Vertically, the center line is 0 dB and the ends are $18$ dB; gain is rounded to 0.1 dB. Horizontally, frequency is $2010^{3x}$ Hz, then clamped to that band's legal range. The far left never goes below 20 Hz; the low third stops at 1000 Hz at the boundary, the middle at 8000 Hz, and the high third at 16000 Hz. Curve dragging does not change eq\_mid\_q; Q stays at its default or whatever value the file already carries.

The low-shelf gain also has a separate slider (eq\_low\_gain); it changes gain only, not the corner frequency. For everyday low-end buildup, the slider is more direct; use the left third of the curve when the corner also needs to move. The master bus can host an EQ too and provides the same low-gain slider; master processing affects the whole project, so finish the display track before touching it.

Common band fixes

Band & Common problem & Direction \\ 20–60 Hz & Sub; too much sounds dull and eats headroom & High-pass or low-shelf cut; first confirm your monitoring can hear it \\ 60–250 Hz & Bass and kick foundation; buildup feels boomy & Gentle low-shelf cut, or separate the parts \\ 250–500 Hz & Low-mid "cardboard" & Narrow 2–3 dB cut; do not hollow out the body \\ 500 Hz–2 kHz & Melody and speech; too much sounds "telephone" & Check arrangement overlap first, then cut gently \\ 2–5 kHz & Presence; too much is harsh and sibilant & Gentle mid-peak cut; boost very sparingly \\ 5–10 kHz & Clarity and sibilance & Small high-shelf moves, with sibilance handling \\ 10 kHz+ & Air & Gentle high-shelf lift; noise rises too \\ Common band problems and directions

These numbers are starting points, not prescriptions. What matters is whether a source still needs that much space in the whole. If two tracks mask each other, solve it with pan, level, or arrangement first; EQ is only one tool.

Boundaries and common mistakes

  • Do not use EQ to fix clipping: a boost changes peaks; clipping belongs to gain structure and dynamics.
  • Do not boost highs on every track: the sum turns harsh; allocate high-end lift across the mix.
  • Do not mix with your eyes: a flat curve is not automatically good, and a curved one is not automatically wrong; return to the full mix.
  • Multiple EQs: only the first curve is drawn: the rack graph follows the first device in the chain that carries EQ parameters. A second EQ still processes, but may not be drawn.
  • Master EQ is global: the master low-shelf gain affects the whole project; do not reach for it to fix a single-track problem.
  • No Q knob in the UI: mid Q exists in the parameter contract, but the current rack has no separate Q control. If a narrower or wider band is needed, this UI cannot do it; do not pretend curve dragging changes Q.

Exercise

  1. Choose a vocal or bass. Push the mid probe temporarily to $+9$ dB and find the muddiest point between 250--500 Hz; return to zero, then cut 2--3 dB.
  2. Move the probe to 2--5 kHz and find the harshest point; cut 1--2 dB without adding a high boost.
  3. Return to the full mix and compare “good in solo” with “good in context.” If the mix becomes thin, halve the cut. Record the final curve and sensation.
