What this volume solves

toc{section}{What this volume solves}

Arrangement and Orchestration{arrangement} answers one practical question: given a melody, a chord progression, or a drum groove, how do you turn it into a complete, layered piece that can be realised in Yeban? It is about what to write, for which voice, and on which layer. It is not about polishing recorded sound; that belongs to the mixing and mastering volume.

The volume assumes you can create a project, add tracks, place clips, and open the piano roll in Yeban. If not, read the User Guide chapters on projects, tracks and clips, and the piano roll first.

Three learning paths

toc{section}{Three learning paths}

  • From zero: read Chapters 1--5 in order and do at least one eight-bar exercise per chapter; finish with the walkthrough in Chapter 10, then read Chapter 11 and pick one standalone genre from Chapters 12--19 to build its eight-bar skeleton.
  • Producer: start with Chapters 2, 3, 4, 8, 10 and 11; consult Chapters 6 and 7 when a string or brass part appears.
  • Orchestrator: read Chapters 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 9 and 11 closely; use Chapters 3 and 5 as support for rhythm and melody.

Writing and review conventions

toc{section}{Writing and review conventions}

  • A term is introduced as “Chinese (English)” on first use and indexed at the end. New terms for this volume are listed in the term table below.
  • UI screenshots use the placeholder |name{caption}|. Names are lowercase and hyphenated; management captures and fills them from the receipt list.
  • Concept figures, flow diagrams and notation examples are drawn in TikZ and shipped with the source. Text in a figure explains relationships; it never pretends to be a real screenshot.
  • Each chapter ends with a help-topic mapping table from section to in-app help anchor. Anchors are lowercase and hyphenated for embedding into the application help.
  • Product claims are limited to entries that exist on the live tip and in the current in-app manual. Unimplemented or planned features are omitted or explicitly marked “not yet available”.

What this volume does not promise

toc{section}{What this volume does not promise}

This volume does not replace mixing, mastering or plug-in manuals. It does not claim that one instrumentation works for every song, and it does not present “professional arranging” as a single correct recipe. Every register, frequency and velocity number is a starting point, not a law. Use it to remove obvious mistakes, then judge with your ears and reference records.
