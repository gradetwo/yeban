Walkthrough: Play the Whole Book Through Two Short Songs

以无厚入有间，恢恢乎其于游刃必有余地矣。

"Zhuangzi", "The Secret of Caring for Life"

Where there is space, a blade with no thickness enters with room to spare. A walkthrough finds the gaps: which layer enters, which one steps aside. Arrangement is not using each chapter once; it is letting them all take their place in one song. A walkthrough goes section by section, puts each layer in, and checks that it does not steal another layer's seat.

The goal: two short songs, one workflow

This book ends without a “universal recipe”. Instead it uses two short songs{short-song} as demo projects{demo-project}: one folk, one pop. Neither is a commercial length; each is sixteen bars (eight-bar verse + eight-bar chorus), but both follow the same workflow:

  1. Write the section chart and energy curve;
  2. Create and name tracks; assign the four layer roles;
  3. Build the rhythm section, then the harmony layer;
  4. Write the lead melody/vocal;
  5. Add colour layers (strings, woodwinds/brass, electronic);
  6. Walk through section by section and fix seat-stealing;
  7. Save the project;
  8. Export WAV and stems, and write a delivery receipt.

Neither demo is a preinstalled project in the repository; both are teaching setups built by the methods in this book. You can rebuild them in Yeban from the tables, or use them only as a checklist.

Demo one: the folk short song “Window Side”

The folk principle is “few but clear”: lead vocal, acoustic guitar, bass, light drums/percussion, string pad. The sixteen-bar section chart:

Track & Type & Role and writing \\ Track & Type & Role and writing \\ Lead vocal & Audio & Narrative; leave one beat at phrase ends, do not fill every gap \\ Acoustic guitar & Audio & Harmonic skeleton; trim clips to leave the vocal's phrase ends \\ Bass & MIDI/audio & Root; mostly same-note repetition, few leaps \\ Drums/percussion & MIDI/audio & Light skeleton; verse keeps kick/snare/light tambourine \\ String pad & MIDI & Enters in the chorus; long notes, not the vocal's rhythm \\ Double/harmony & Audio & Enters after bar 13; low third, not in the lead's register \\

The folk energy curve rises gently; do not turn the chorus into electronic dance music. The chorus is “bigger” because of the string pad, fuller drums and one double, not because of more tracks.

Demo two: the pop short song “Night Run”

The pop principle is “each layer clearer”, not “each layer more”. Sixteen bars:

Bars & Section & Arrangement action \\ Bars & Section & Arrangement action \\ 1--8 & Verse & Drums + bass + pad + lead; pluck only at phrase ends \\ 9--12 & First chorus & Add double, continuous pluck, brass stab at bar 9 \\ 13--16 & Second chorus & Add harmony and stack in the last two bars; brass stops after the stab \\

Track & Type & Role and writing \\ Track & Type & Role and writing \\ Lead vocal & Audio & Lyric first; lift in the chorus but stay in a comfortable register \\ Double & Audio & Same melody, second take; not a copy of the lead \\ Harmony & Audio & A third below the lead; chorus only \\ Drums & MIDI & Four-beat skeleton; add density in the chorus \\ Bass & MIDI & Root; divide the low end with sub \\ Pad & MIDI & Midrange bed; open cutoff in the chorus \\ Pluck & MIDI & High decoration; phrase ends/gaps, not continuous \\ Brass & MIDI & Short stab at bar 9; exit after a beat and a half \\

The pop chorus is not “open every track”; it is “each layer more complete”: fuller drums, steadier bass, brighter pad, clearer pluck, thicker vocal. The track count can stay the same while every role becomes more definite.

Energy curves of the two short songs: folk rises gently, pop lifts more clearly from verse to chorus; neither is a flat line.

Eight-step walkthrough

Step 1: section chart and energy curve

Write two tables first: a section chart (bars, section, action) and a track chart (track, type, role). Without them, every added track becomes “add first, decide later”.

Step 2: create and name tracks

Use the role cards from chapter 1: rhythm, harmony, melody, colour. Name tracks so a human can read them (“Lead”, “Bass”, “Pad”), not “Track 1” and “Track 2”.

Step 3: rhythm section

Build drums and bass first: drums define time and groove, bass defines the root. Solo both and confirm kick and bass root do not fight on the same beat. Folk drums stay light; pop drums can be fuller, but neither should fill every sixteenth.

Step 4: harmony layer

Add acoustic guitar/keyboard/pad: write the chord movement first, then choose the texture. Folk uses fingerpicking/strumming; pop uses a pad bed. Do not start with five harmony tracks.

Step 5: lead melody and vocal

Write the lead or record the vocal: make the lyric clear and leave phrase ends open. Folk can rely on the guitar; pop needs double/harmony behind the lead. Never let backing cover the lead.

Step 6: colour layers

Add strings, woodwinds/brass or electronic pluck by section: enter only when colour/energy is needed, and be able to exit. Both short songs use one or two colours, not every instrument in the book.

Step 7: section-by-section walkthrough

Solo/Mute through the section chart: does every section enter and exit? Is only one layer leading the low end? Is the lyric clear? Is there a fill/space at the transition? Answer at least once per section; do not just play the whole song once.

Step 8: save and deliver

Save the project, export WAV and stems, write the receipt. Problems found in step 7 go back to the relevant track; do not force them down with faders.

Save, export and deliver

  1. Save the project: use File $$ Save project (Ctrl+S) to write back to the same file, or File $$ Save as (Ctrl+Shift+S) to a new name. Saving is an atomic write{atomic-write}; a power cut halfway does not leave half a file.
  2. Version history: use Edit $$ Arrangement version time machine to view the commit chain; click any version node to return to it. This is project editing history, not a mix version.
  3. Export WAV: use File $$ Export audio (WAV)... to set output path, sample rate, bit depth, duration and the bus limiter. The receipt reports frames, true peak and limiter work.
  4. Export stems: use File $$ Export stems... to export each track separately; the receipt reports how many were exported, skipped and the output directory.
  5. Do not confuse the diagnostic bundle: Diagnostics $$ Export project diagnostic report bundle is for troubleshooting, not audio; do not deliver it as a WAV.
  6. Write the delivery receipt: record project file, WAV, stem directory, sample rate/bit depth, export time and skipped items. Without a receipt, nobody can reproduce the delivery.

Exercise

Choose folk or pop and build sixteen bars in Yeban:

  1. Write a section chart (eight-bar verse + eight-bar chorus) and a track chart (no more than eight tracks).
  2. Follow the eight steps: rhythm $$ harmony $$ lead/vocal $$ colour; solo after adding each track.
  3. Tick every item on the walkthrough checklist; fix at least one “seat-stealing” problem (low end, lead register or a colour layer that never exits).
  4. Save the project, export WAV and stems, and write a delivery receipt; keep the project file, WAV, stem directory and receipt in one folder.

When finished, answer one question: if you could keep only five tracks, which three would you delete? If you can answer clearly, you understand what each layer does.

Chapter takeaways

  • A walkthrough is not “listen again”; it checks entrances, exits and seat-stealing layer by layer against the section chart.
  • Both short songs share one workflow: section chart $$ track chart $$ rhythm $$ harmony $$ vocal $$ colour $$ check $$ deliver.
  • Folk is few but clear; pop is each layer clearer. Different styles need different track charts.
  • The chorus is “big” because every layer is more complete, not because faders or track counts are higher.
  • Saving is atomic; WAV and stems are two deliveries; the diagnostic bundle is not the work.
  • Delivery must include a receipt: project, WAV, stems, parameters and skipped items can all be checked.
