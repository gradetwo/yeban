Automation and Modulation

易穷则变，变则通，通则久。

Book of Changes, “Appended Judgments II” (《周易·系辞下》)

Translation: “When a path is exhausted, it changes; through change, it flows; through flow, it endures.” Automation lets parameters change over time; with change, the sound stays alive.

Automation lets parameters change over time: draw a curve, or record your movements while playing back. This chapter covers showing lanes, what the four write modes mean, and the real boundary of modulation in the current build.

Automation Curves and Write Modes

Show the lanes

Press A in the arrangement area, or use the View automation switch (Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+A) to show automation lanes. Each non-master track can have its own lanes, arranged by target parameter. A lane holds a polyline whose vertices are connected in time order; its horizontal axis aligns with the arrangement timeline, so you can read the curve directly against clip positions.

Draw and edit the curve

A lane's polyline is made of vertices. Adding one gives the parameter a value at a point in time; neighbouring vertices are interpolated. Drag a vertex to change its time or value; delete one and the curve re-connects across the gap. Curves are saved with the project, drive parameters during playback and take effect on export too.

The four write modes

Yeban's model defines four automation write modes; knowing them avoids recording curves you did not mean to:

  • [Off] The default. Nothing is written during playback; manual parameter changes do not alter the curve.
  • [Write] Once the parameter is changed during playback, writing continues until you explicitly stop.
  • [Touch] Writes only while you are physically touching the parameter; releasing stops immediately. Good for a single corrective nudge.
  • [Latch] Once touched, writing continues even after you release, until explicitly stopped. Good for locking in a whole fader move.

    • Write modes compared Mode & After you release \\ Off & No writing \\ Write & Keeps writing (explicit stop) \\ Touch & Stops immediately \\ Latch & Keeps writing (explicit stop) \\

    Parameter Binding and LFO

    Bind a parameter

    An automation lane binds to a specific parameter on a specific track: choose the target, then draw on that lane. Device parameters (e.g.\ on an insert effect) can also be automation targets; insert the device, then pick its parameter in the lane's target list. Parameter changes and automation writes share one commit path, so undo restores them step by step — you will not get a curve change that the project failed to record.

    The real LFO boundary

    Yeban's DSP core does implement a global LFO (low-frequency oscillator): one shared by all voices, evaluated per block, with selectable waveforms. But note carefully: it is not yet wired into the synthesizer's modulation inputs. Do not expect to turn an LFO knob in the GUI and get vibrato or filter sweep; those modulation paths are still evolving.

    Automation flow and the LFO boundary: solid = available path, dashed = modulation not yet wired

    For that reason this chapter does not describe vibrato or filter sweep as available features. If you cannot find the control in the GUI, it is not wired up yet — you have not missed it.
