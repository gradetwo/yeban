The Piano Roll

凡音之起，由人心生也。

Book of Rites, “Record of Music” (《礼记·乐记》)

Translation: “All music begins in the human heart.” Before a note lands on the roll there is an emotion to express; technique and rules serve what is heard.

The piano roll is where MIDI notes are written: time runs horizontally, pitch vertically. This chapter covers opening it, drawing notes, editing velocity and quantizing, plus the current limits of note dragging.

Open and Navigate the View

Open the roll

Press P, or switch to the piano roll — the first bottom-console tab; F7 is the direct key for that tab. Select a clip on a MIDI synth track and the roll shows the notes in that clip.

Read the two dimensions

The horizontal axis is time (in ticks, converted to bars/beats from the project's time signature); the vertical axis is pitch, arranged as pitch lanes from high to low, with key labels on the left (e.g.\ C4, Eb5) as references. A readout at the top shows the currently visible pitch range. At the bottom is the velocity lane, where each note's velocity appears as a vertical bar.

Zoom and framing

Cmd/Ctrl+Up/Down zooms tracks vertically; Cmd/Ctrl+=/-- zooms the timeline horizontally. The roll has a "framing" notion: you can frame the current clip, the current track or all content. Pitches outside the visible window are not drawn — change the framing to see them.

Note Entry, Selection and Movement

Draw notes

The onboarding tour puts it plainly: number key 2 is the draw tool; click on the roll and a note lands there. The drop position comes from the click: horizontal for time, vertical resolved from the y coordinate into a pitch lane. Once drawn, drag the note to change time or pitch, or drag its right edge to change length.

Select and drag

Clicking a note selects it. In the current version pointer dragging acts only on the note under the pointer — even if several are selected, dragging one leaves the others where they are. So "group drag" and "group duration change" do not work yet; move notes one at a time, or wait until the capability officially lands. Yeban does not disguise an unimplemented operation as a success.

Common roll flow: select clip $$ draw $$ edit pitch/length $$ quantize $$ audition

Velocity, Quantize and Scale Tools

Velocity

In the bottom velocity lane each note has a vertical bar whose height is its velocity. You can edit a note's velocity directly there; the roll previews the value while dragging.

Quantize and humanize

Edit $$ Note Quantize (Quantize 1/16)... snaps selected notes to a 1/16 grid; Edit $$ Note Humanize... opens a dialog with time jitter, velocity jitter, strength and seed settings to make mechanical results feel more natural. Both affect only the current selection.

Scale and chord tools

The current version offers key labels on the pitch lanes as pitch references; whether standalone scale/chord generation tools exist depends on the build you use — do not assume an entry that is not present. Edit $$ Drum Generation (Drums)... does exist and can generate a drum clip from style, complexity and seed.

Step Editing and Chord Assistance

"Step editing" here means writing notes one fixed step at a time: place a note in each target cell with the draw tool, then use quantize to tidy timing. It is not a one-click melody generator.

For chords, the available approach is to draw several pitches stacked at the same moment and verify by ear; automatic chord-progression generation is outside this chapter's scope and is not described as available.

Hands-on exercise Open the roll on a MIDI track clip, draw a one-bar C-major scale with number key 2, make the first note stronger in the velocity lane, quantize with Note Quantize to 1/16, then press Space to audition; finally drag one note and confirm the others stay put.
